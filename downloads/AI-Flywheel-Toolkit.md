# The Flywheel Toolkit — 28 tools, free to use

> Every template, checklist, and diagnostic in the book, stripped to its working form.
> Honest scoring only: a flattering score is a wasted tool. It is almost never the model —
> it is data readiness, implementation discipline, workflow redesign, and governance.

---

## 1 · Orient & Locate

### 01 · The Four-Leaks Diagnostic  (Ch 1)
Mark each leak PLUGGED or LEAKING. Value drains at the FIRST leak.
- [ ] LEAK 1 — DATA READINESS: was the data ready, clean, usable?
- [ ] LEAK 2 — UNDEFINED SUCCESS: defined & measurable BEFORE building?
- [ ] LEAK 3 — WORKFLOW INTEGRATION: redesigned, or AI bolted on the old one?
- [ ] LEAK 4 — GOVERNANCE: could you trust/deploy it where it mattered?

### 02 · Locate Your Organization on the Flywheel  (Ch 3)
Which stages are live (Foundation/Generative/Agentic/Physical)? Is each solid? Do wins compound, or reset (the reset tax)? Next turn = strengthen the lowest solid stage.

### 03 · The AI Flywheel Maturity Model  (Ch 23)
Levels: 1 Absent · 2 Ad hoc · 3 Emerging · 4 Disciplined · 5 Compounding — across Stages / DRIVE / CORE. Your level = your LOWEST pillar.

### 04 · The AI Flywheel Self-Assessment  (Ch 23)
Score 1–5 across Stages (S1–S3), DRIVE (D1–D4), CORE (C1–C3). Your level = lowest pillar average; your next move = your single lowest-scoring question.

## 2 · Diagnose

### 05 · The Opportunity Selection Matrix  (Ch 9)
Score candidates 1–5 on Volume, Measurability, Data-available. Moonshots score low on data and measurability — which is why they fail first.

### 06 · The Data-Readiness Checklist (the Gate)  (Ch 4/9)
EXISTS · CLEAN · CONNECTED · RELEVANT · ALLOWED · FRESH. Any "no" = stop and fix the data first.

### 07 · The Success-Definition One-Pager  (Ch 9)
What we're improving · Baseline → Target · The one metric that proves it · By when · Owner (a name). If you can't fill it, you're not ready to build.

### 08 · The Fast-First-Value Selection Grid  (Ch 5)
Ship the high-value, high-ease/safe generative quick win in ~30 days. GenAI is the ante, not the edge.

### 09 · The Physical AI Candidate Screen  (Ch 7)
Repetitive & high-volume · Simulatable · High-value · Boundable. Mostly yes = a candidate.

## 3 · Resource & Build

### 10 · The Build-vs-Buy Decision Aid  (Ch 10)
Road or car? · Can we build to production on time? · Lock-in cost? · Time-to-value? Build & own the road, rent the car, partner for delivery.

### 11 · The Architecture-Neutrality Checklist  (Ch 10)
Model-swappable · Open standards · Data-portable · No choke-point · Knowledge kept · Exit-costed. Any "no" = a bar of a cage.

### 12 · The Agent Operating Model  (Ch 6)
Agent · Owner · Goal · Tools · Permissions · Checkpoints · Escalation · Kill-switch. Plan for the deployment, not the demo.

### 13 · The Implementation Readiness Checklist  (Ch 11)
Technically works · Workflow redesigned · Reliable & watched · Integrated · Human role set · Change-managed. Passing only #1 = a demo.

## 4 · Validate & Expand

### 14 · The Validation Scorecard  (Ch 12)
Metric / Baseline / Target / Actual / Verdict / Audited-by. The skeptic's test: would the finance manager at the back accept this as proof?

### 15 · The Honest-ROI Horizon  (Ch 12)
Back-office ~6–18 months · Generative ~1–2 yrs · Agentic ~3–5 yrs · Physical longest. Set the horizon before you build.

### 16 · The Pilot-to-Platform Expansion Checklist  (Ch 13)
Standardized · Governed · Reusable · Composable · Telemetry · Champion · Owned. A "no" on 1, 2, or 6 = a hero's dish, not a recipe.

### 17 · The Flywheel Handoff Map  (Ch 8)
For each stage, name what it produces that fuels the next (the baton: Data · Capability · Trust · Capital). The race is won in the handoffs.

### 18 · The Next-Turn Worksheet  (Ch 2)
This win added what to Data / Capability / Trust? Therefore the next turn is… A win that feeds nothing pays the reset tax.

## 5 · Govern (CORE)

### 19 · The Governance Maturity Quick Scan  (Ch 14)
Can you see it? Who owns it? Can you stop it? Lawful & fair? Human in the loop? Leadership owns it? Your weakest row holds your next deployment down.

### 20 · The Compliance & Sovereignty Readiness Checklist  (Ch 15)
Risk-classified · Designed-for · Fairness-tested · Residency known · Sovereign where required · Provable. Answer on the blueprint, not after inspection.

### 21 · The Agent Accountability Register  (Ch 16)
One row per agent: Purpose · Named owner · Autonomy/permissions · Traceable? · Escalation · Kill-switch tested? No row, no deployment.

### 22 · The Autonomy-Level Decision Guide  (Ch 17)
Stakes × Reversibility × Ambiguity → Full auto / Auto+notify / Human-in-the-loop / Human decides. When in doubt, set it one notch lower.

## 6 · Choose Your Starting Point

### 23 · The Business Starting-Point Selector  (Ch 18)
Score Volume · Cost · Measurability · Data-readiness · Reversibility. Start high-volume, high-cost, measurable, data-ready, forgiving.

### 24 · The Public-Sector Use-Case Prioritizer  (Ch 19)
Score Reach · Pain · Workforce gap · Measurability · Data & sovereignty, then weigh governance burden. Reach is the return; the road must be sovereign.

### 25 · The Mission-Impact Use-Case Selector  (Ch 20)
Score Mission leverage · Capacity freed · Measurability · Data & ethics, then weigh trust at stake. Turn overhead into mission; ethics leads.

### 26 · The Economic Flywheel Design Template  (Ch 21)
Capability → Compute → Deployment → Value → Reinvestment. An economy runs on implementers, not intelligence. Start where your fuel is.

## 7 · Start & Sustain

### 27 · The 90-Day Flywheel Start Plan  (Ch 22)
Days 1–30 Diagnose · Days 31–60 Resource + Implement · Days 61–90 Validate + Expand. The whole book on one page. Commit a Day 1 and an owner.

### 28 · The Quarterly Refresh Cadence  (Ch 24)
Hold constant: the flywheel, DRIVE, CORE, "own the road." Refresh quarterly: which model leads, what's newly possible, your assumptions. Update the fuel, never the engine.
